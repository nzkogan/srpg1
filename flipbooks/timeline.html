Chronicle of the Two Claims

Eight hundred years of a record nobody can fully read, and the campaign that walks through the last forty. Built from the canon workbook; gold marks the three memories you play, green what the game already does, red what is still waiting to be written.

I. The record

Years are counted back from the year Act 1 opens. Spacing is by era, not to scale.

about 800 years before

The Hundred Cities

Every valley held a city-state with its own patron, temple and king. The patrons were named parties with terms, not metaphors.

Memory F0 · prologue

Ur-Nashet, the last night

The first battle of the game is 800 years early, in a city that no longer exists. Eight ancestors, each a preview of a class you will spend thirty chapters earning. The city is taken and its patron is written into a book, and you did that. The name is never shown: the scribe's hand covers the page, the angle is wrong, the rain is on it.

built: the prologueno permadeath · chariots · one weapon each
about 780 years before

The Binding of Sargath

Sargath of Ur-Nashet binds the rest. He did not kill the patrons; he wrote them down, which turned gods into signatories. Every pact in the present is a clause in a treaty nobody alive negotiated.

The First Register

A register is written

Which patron owes what to whom. It outlived his empire by seven centuries. The demons the nobles borrow from are deposed city-patrons, and the register is his instrument.

Memory F1 · between Act 1 and Act 2

The scribes' quarter

The Register is copied for the first time, under deadline. Five defenders hold a walled quarter; four copyists sit at their desks and three must live eight turns. A clerk writes each deed down under the doer's name, which teaches the epithet ledger. When the copy is finished a page is lost and nothing ever says which. The missing name enters the world here, as an accident nobody notices.

built: copyists · clerk ledger · the lost page
780 to 90 years before

The Long Reign

One realm, one church, and the church that held it together was not telling the truth. Learning moved east and came back translated; the capital lived on inheritance. This is the age the Diadem still thinks it is living in.

Memory F2 · between Act 2 and Act 3

The last court of the Long Reign

The church that held the realm together, on the day it stopped being believed. Six defenders face fourteen levies, no reinforcements, no supply. Every pact answers once, free, and the court falls regardless. Hold to dawn or lose the company first: either way the memory counts, and losing is its ending.

built: free pact · the court falls
about 90 to 40 years before

The Venerated

A person, and then an absence. What they did is disputed, whether they died is disputed, and so is the tomb. No depiction survives that all parties accept as authentic. The sects are five answers to one question the game never settles.

The calendar schism

When was the Venerated born?

That fixes the sign, the governing decan, the feast kept and which spirits are properly invoked. The court keeps the Ram (spring, born at the lambing). The Continuing Word says the Vessel (winter, and the date can move). The Company of the Road says the Scales (autumn, the reckoning season). The Unstained hold no feast valid. The silent will not say, and they are the only ones who might know.

built: each sect's calendar sets which unit signs a chapter favours
Unwritten

The five answers

Canon fixes the question and the sects' stances but not what each sect says the Venerated did. Writing the five answers, each credible and none provable, is the heart of the setting.

needs authoring
40 to 1 years before

The War of the Two Claims

The realm splits: crowned succession against elective succession, and both call themselves the inheritance. It ended in exhaustion, not victory. The claim did not end.

The wounds

What the war and the plague left

Each sect carries one. The Vashti men: the lords who hosted the experiments still hold office. The March voices: plague, and the last hierarch's silence. The Company of the Road: starvation, when the war closed the road, and a stranger who extended credit to people who could not repay. The court: proximity to power. The silent: closest to the truth, least interested in sharing it.

Year 0

Act 1 opens the year after

The Diadem (crowned, antique, the largest library on the continent, almost none of it originally theirs) and the Assembly (elective, socially current: contract law, property held by women) are two exhausted co-claimants.

Act 2

The columns

The army splits by sect and by supply. Each stop is a seat of one sect's answer to the same question.

Chapter 11 · both roads

The Second Writ

The largest map, the whole army deployed once. The commission splits. The church surveyor is revealed as the crown's auditor, and the deputy is named off the epithet ledger: the crown's pick is accepted, or contested once, which the crown grants and pays for by withholding Column B's supply. Eight candidates are scored on witnessed deeds, objectives and kills.

III. Threads waiting for a writer

Where canon stops and the story could begin. Each names the tab it comes from, so a decision lands in the workbook.

eras, sects, calendar

The Venerated: What did they do, and did they die?

Five sects answer; none can be proven. Each answer needs a voice, a relic and a reason to be believed.

flashbacks

The withheld name: What Nashar wrote

Never shown, by design. What can be written is everyone who has argued about it since. Treat it as a constraint to write around, and the page lost in the scribes' quarter as the thing that made it possible.

guest_units, calendar

Anna's payoff: The decan token

What it proves, who contests it, and how it reaches Act 3. Canon marks it a reconstruction gap and the calendar schism depends on it.

the_claimant

The Enlightened One: A name, a history, whether he believes it

The only decision made is that his horror is generosity. Everything else is open.

the_hierophant

The Hierophant: Beyond Methodios

The prophet is known; the person he speaks for is not.

ascension_signals

The ascension signals: Which five things the game watches

Dispersal against kills, the Unstained respected or steamrolled, Kest's restitution, how forge access was used, how Maren was handled. Candidates, not a locked list.

units, ascension_signals

Kest: Restitution or one more extraction

A prodigious thief raised in a guild, recruited by heist. How the decan-token thread resolves for her is a signal in the ending.

scenes

The scripts: Thirty-seven scenes, no words

Every scene is drafted with a summary and a word count of zero. The scene screen is ready to show a script the moment one exists.

chapters

The war arc: How many fronts, and where

Canon says 'several'; three chapters were invented to hold the shape.

units

Looks: What anyone looks like

Canon records stats and roles, not appearances. A short note per character, even a line, would give the next art pass something to hold.
